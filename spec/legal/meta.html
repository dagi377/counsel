1. Separate product, built on Paploo

Own repo, own releases, own database. Paploo supplies the agent runtime: per-firm containers, credentials, egress control, audit, approvals, Slack and web chat. No legal code goes into Paploo.

Decided

2. Agent works, a person approves

Every action that reaches a client, a court, a bank or the trust ledger stops at a gate. The agent prepares and cites its sources; a named person approves, and the approval is logged.

Decided

3. One isolated cell per firm

Each firm gets its own namespace, database, keys and agent containers. Privileged data never shares a table with another firm. Fine at tens of firms; revisit at hundreds.

Needs your OK

4. Data stays in Canada

Hosting, backups and every model call in Canadian regions for every firm, with the inference region logged per call. Clio also offers a Canadian region (opt-in), so the edge is "always, including AI processing, provable". We claim it only once Canadian-region inference is contracted.

Needs your OK

5. Own design, Clio-level coverage

We match what Clio does, screen by screen, but with our own layout and words. Copying their pages or branding is a legal risk.

Decided

6. Work tracking built in, Jira optional

Firms track work as items on boards inside the app. A connector can mirror items to an external Jira for firms that already use it. Note: Clio Work is an AI research and drafting workspace, not a task tracker; our Work area compares with Clio Manage tasks and workflows.

Needs your OK

7. Ontario first

LSO by-laws, Ontario court rules and limitation periods are built in. Other provinces later, as rule packs.

Needs your OK

8. Admins run the system, not the files

Firm admin manages users, roles and settings but does not automatically see privileged matters or move trust money. Matter access follows the team; trust authority belongs only to named licensees; nobody approves their own work; the agent never approves.

Needs your OK

9. Paploo gets a generic "apps" hook

An app declares its gates, skills and data access; Paploo runs the agent, shows the gate and keeps the audit trail. Counsel is the first app. Designed separately, nothing built yet.

Needs your OK

Two products, one agent runtime. Each firm is a sealed cell.

What lives where

Counsel

  • Legal data model: matters, contacts, time, invoices, trust ledgers
  • Screens in this spec
  • Gate engine: who may approve what, and the record of it
  • Ontario rule packs: deadlines, trust, ID checks
  • Legal skills the agent uses

Paploo

  • Runs the agent safely, per user
  • Holds connection secrets (email, bank feed, Jira, e-sign)
  • Egress control and audit of every agent action
  • Chat surfaces: Slack and web
  • Budgets and cost per firm

Outside services (via connectors)

  • Email (Microsoft 365 or Google)
  • E-signature
  • Payments and bank feeds
  • Jira (optional)
  • QuickBooks Online Canada or Xero (two-way, trust summary only)
  • Model provider (Canadian-region inference)

Rules that do not bend

  • No money moves, no document is filed or sent, and no deadline is trusted without a person's approval.
  • Every agent answer cites its source: document, page and line.
  • Trust withdrawals need an authorized lawyer; the ledger can never go below zero.
  • Firm data never leaves the firm's cell or Canada; every model call records its provider and region.
  • Vendor support sees firm data only under a time-limited grant from the owner.
